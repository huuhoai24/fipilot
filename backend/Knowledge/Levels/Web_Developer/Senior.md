# Web Developer - Senior Level Knowledge Depth & Evaluation

### Knowledge Depth

The candidate should understand:

- The principles and limitations of web architecture at organizational and large-product scale.
- How to translate business objectives, user needs and quality attributes into web architecture decisions.
- Decision frameworks for selecting technologies, rendering strategies and platform capabilities.
- Trade-offs among performance, scalability, reliability, security, accessibility, maintainability, delivery speed and cost.
- Frontend architecture patterns such as monolithic frontend, modular frontend, micro-frontends and Backend for Frontend.
- The relationship among web clients, backend services, APIs, identity systems, data platforms, CDNs and edge infrastructure.
- Platform-level concerns such as design systems, shared libraries, developer experience and frontend governance.
- API governance, contract management, compatibility and dependency lifecycle.
- Security architecture, trust boundaries, identity, privacy, content security policy and software supply-chain risk.
- Performance governance, performance budgets, capacity planning and global delivery strategies.
- Accessibility governance, regulatory obligations and inclusive design at organizational scale.
- Reliability concepts such as graceful degradation, fault isolation, resilience, SLI, SLO and production monitoring.
- Build-versus-buy, standardization-versus-flexibility and centralization-versus-team autonomy trade-offs.
- Migration strategy, legacy modernization, transition architecture and operational risk.
- Technical debt, long-term maintainability, total cost of ownership and organizational scalability.
- When not to adopt micro-frontends, heavy client-side frameworks, server-side rendering or other complex architectures.
- How to challenge solution assumptions before committing to architectural decisions.
- The long-term impact of web technology decisions on users, products, teams and business capabilities.

### Evaluation Focus

- Principles and limitations.
- Architecture.
- Decision frameworks.
- Multi-dimensional trade-offs.
- Governance.
- Security and privacy.
- Performance and reliability.
- Accessibility at scale.
- Cost and technical debt.
- Long-term implications.
- Systems thinking.
- Ability to challenge assumptions.
